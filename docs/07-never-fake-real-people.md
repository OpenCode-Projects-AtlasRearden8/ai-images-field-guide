# Never Fake Real People

This is the chapter I'd put on the cover if covers could have rules: **never make an AI image of a real person without their genuine permission — and some uses never get permission, no matter what.**

## The line, in one sentence

If an image makes a viewer believe a real, specific person said, did, wore, or endorsed something they didn't — it's a deepfake. Don't make it, don't share it, don't "just joke" with it.

## Zero-tolerance zone

Not gray, not situational. Never make images that put real people into:

- **Anything sexual, nude, or intimate** — no exceptions, no "it's just for a laugh," and this includes celebrities and people you know.
- **Fake news or fake events** — an image of a real person at a protest they never attended, signing a document they never signed.
- **Scams** — fake endorsements, fake "I need help, wire money" photos.
- **Fake IDs or credentials.**
- **Anything that mocks or demeans** a specific real person.

Whether a tool *technically lets* you generate such an image is irrelevant. Some do. That's an indictment of the tool, not permission for you.

## What about my own family? See Chapter 8.

Yes, you can make grandma's 80th birthday poster — with *her* permission or your family's blessing, as a clearly AI-stylized artwork, never passing a fake photo off as a real event. Chapter 8 is the full etiquette.

## Fictional people: fine, and say so

You can absolutely generate an imaginary woman named Dana who runs an imaginary bakery for your novel's website. Do one thing first: **label her as AI-generated or fictional** wherever she appears. A caption costs you nothing and protects your reader's trust.

:::caution
**The share test:** if you post it, would a reasonable person scrolling by believe it was a real photograph of a real event? If yes — label it ("AI-generated image," "fictional character") or don't post. Your credibility compounds; each honest label makes the next post more believable too.
:::

## Why your own face is the safest face

Making AI portraits of *yourself* is generally the cleanest case (it's your likeness), with two caveats: check whether your tool stores/trains on uploads (some do by default — turn it off), and remember that even flattering fakes can mislead: don't post an AI "photo" of yourself finishing a marathon you didn't run.

## If you slip — or someone fakes you

- If you made something you shouldn't have: delete it everywhere, apologize directly if a person was affected, and move on corrected.
- If someone faked *you*: screenshot everything (dates, URLs, what it shows), report it to the platform, and tell people you trust. Most platforms treat fake likeness reports seriously now.

> One more time for the people in the back: no fake nudes, no fake news, no fake endorsements, no exceptions dressed up as jokes. Everything else in this guide is technique; this chapter is character.
