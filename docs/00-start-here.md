# Start Here

Welcome! This is a field guide for making pictures with AI tools: written like a friend would explain it. Plain English, step by step, never condescending. If you've ever typed something into a chat box and gotten a wall of jargon back, this is the opposite of that.

## Who this is for

People who want to make pictures: birthday cards, book covers, blog headers, party invitations, a logo sketch to show a designer, a poster for the church potluck. You don't need to code, you don't need an art degree, and you don't need to spend money to follow along.

## The quick version (for the impatient)

Everything in this guide comes down to five habits:

- **Ask with details.** "A photo of X" gives you a lottery ticket; "a photo of X, lit like this, framed like that" gives you a plan.
- **Change one thing at a time** when editing or re-rolling. Small changes, small surprises.
- **It won't be right on the first try.** Plan for 3 to 10 attempts. That's normal, not failure.
- **Know the rules.** Check the tool's terms, never fake a real person, and label AI images when you share them.
- **Check before you print.** Resolution, margins, and a test print on cheap paper first.

## What you'll need

- Any device with a web browser. A phone works; a laptop is more comfortable.
- Optionally: one paid or free AI image tool of your choice. This guide stays tool-neutral on purpose, because tools change their pricing and rules every few months.

## Two promises

This guide will not waste your time with hype, and it will not help you do things that hurt people: no fake photos of real people, no pretending someone else's art style is your product, no sneaky watermark removal. The safety chapters are part of the manual, not an appendix at the back.

> Note: This guide is education, not legal advice. When something touches law (copyright, likeness, printing contracts), I'll tell you where to look, not what a lawyer would say. Chapter 6 covers where to look.
