# Family-Photo Respect

Families give us our best material — and our biggest trust responsibilities. This chapter is the etiquette of making AI art from or with your family's photos. The theme in one line: **their faces are their data, and their memories are not raw material for experiments.**

## Rule 1: Consent, every time, even for family

"Family" is not a standing permission slip. Before you:

- upload a family member's photo to *any* AI tool,
- generate a stylized version of their face,
- put an AI-processed image of them on a card, shirt, or public post,

...ask. Actually ask. "Hey, want me to make a fun painted version of that beach photo?" is a 10-second text that saves a relationship scar. This goes double (and triple) for anyone who can't easily say no: kids, elders, someone in a hard season.

## Rule 2: Think about where the photo goes

When you upload a family photo to a web-based tool, it leaves the house. The prompt, the faces, the metadata — off to servers you don't control. Some tools keep or train on uploads by default.

- Check the tool's privacy/training settings and turn **upload-training off** if available.
- For family faces, prefer tools that say they don't store uploads, or local/offline tools that never send anything anywhere.
- If a tool's privacy page is vague, treat the photo as public the moment it's uploaded — and let that inform whether it should be uploaded at all.

:::caution
**Kids get extra protection.** Children can't consent and can't un-share. Be conservative: prefer local-only tools for kids' images, never post AI-manipulated photos of other people's children without their parents' explicit OK, and never anything that a stranger could mistake for a real private photo of a child.
:::

## Rule 3: Never pass AI edits off as real memories

The line that matters most: if you AI-style a family photo, don't let anyone believe it's the *original* moment. Options that keep trust intact:

- Label it: "AI-stylized from our 2019 beach trip."
- Keep the original photo archived and share both, original first.
- Never restore "lost" memories by inventing them — that AI-restored photo of grandma's wedding is a *loving guess*, not evidence, and presenting it as real can genuinely hurt (imagine a relative correcting the record at the funeral).

A gorgeous fake memory shared as real is a small lie that compounds. A gorgeous fake memory shared *as art* is a gift.

## Rule 4: Grief and memorials — handle with both hands

AI can make a touching tribute image of someone who has passed. Done respectfully, with family consent, and clearly labeled as AI: this can be a real comfort. Done carelessly: putting a smiling AI version of grandpa on a public page without asking his children first is a wound. If you're grieving and tempted to generate "just one more picture," that's human — but make the *first* call to the family, not to the tool.

## Rule 5: The family group chat is not a marketplace

Please don't sell AI portraits of your relatives to relatives, or submit AI-styled family photos into contests that don't allow AI entries. That's not respect for family or for contest rules. If you make something a cousin wants to buy, sell *new commissioned art*, not their face.

:::tip
**A two-minute family photo habit:** keep a folder of originals untouched (`family-photos-originals/`), and a folder for anything AI-stylized (`family-photos-ai-stylized/`, filenames include the year and "AI"). In five years, nobody has to wonder which memories are real.
:::

> Bottom line: ask first, know where the photo travels, label the art, protect the kids, handle grief gently. Your family will forgive a bad AI render; they won't forget a violated trust.
